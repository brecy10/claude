## Q1. Which polymers have been used or proposed as TGV/TPV liners, buffer layers, via-fills or glass-surface buffers, and by whom, when and where?

### Takeaway
Hardware evidence is concentrated in Georgia Tech PRC's 2010–2019 glass-interposer program. That program used polymer-laminated thin glass, with ZEON ZIF/ZEONIF, Rogers RXP-4, ABF GX92P and GY11 and epoxy-type primers, along with polymer-filled "via-in-via" TPVs. Most other work is FEA. Examples are Zhao et al. 2022 (HD4100 polyimide buffer), Ahmed et al. 2020/21, Sharma/Dixit/Manhas 2026, Le & Choa 2024/2025 and the 电子与封装 2025 special issue. Parylene (HT/AF4/F) and BCB for TGVs appear mainly in vendor material, patents (BCB coaxial TGV CN111739870A) and TSV analogues, not in peer-reviewed TGV reliability data. Intel filed a dedicated "Polymer TGV buffer layer" glass-core application, published in 2026.

### Inferences
- The polymers break into three families. The first is (a) glass-surface laminates, which also enter the via in via-first or tented schemes: ZIF/ZEONIF, RXP-4, ABF GX92P/GY11, epoxy primers (GT 2011–2019). The second is (b) via-sidewall liners, nearly all simulation or patent: HD4100 PI, PI-2610/2611-type PI, BCB, Parylene HT/AF4/F. The third is (c) polymer via-fill with re-drilled Cu ("via-in-via", GT patent and JMEP 2019; Zhao 2022 "controlled polymer filling"). The most mature and production-like route is resin plugging of conformal-Cu TGVs in glass-core substrates, but I found no stress-buffer-specific public data on it.
- I found no peer-reviewed experimental paper that isolates a parylene or BCB sidewall liner in a Cu-filled TGV and reports crack statistics. The parylene/BCB TGV evidence base is vendor, patent and TSV-analogue material.
- Industry interest became visible around 2024–2026 (Intel CN/DE/US filings). This suggests that polymer buffers are being considered for glass-core HVM. Publication of an application does not show adoption.

- I could not retrieve the full texts of Zhao 2022, Demir TDMR 2017 or 电子与封装 070103. So the exact polymer grade, thickness and via dimensions in Demir TDMR 2017 remain unknown. My best inference from the 2017 TCPMT paper is a ~5 µm polymer on 100 µm glass with 10–20 µm vias, which is (unverified) for TDMR.

## Q2. Deposition and integration methods, and their limits: thickness and uniformity versus via diameter and aspect ratio

### Takeaway
Four integration routes exist: vacuum lamination of B-staged films (ABF, ZIF, epoxy primers), which tent or partly flow into vias; polymer via-fill followed by laser re-drill (via-in-via); spin or vacuum-assisted coating (BCB, PI); and room-temperature vapor-phase CVD (parylene). Demonstrated conformal parylene liners are about 1 µm thick at AR ≤ 10 in TSV analogues with >80% step coverage. Reported TGV polymer buffer thicknesses range from 1 µm (电子与封装 2025) to 5 µm (GT primer) to 10 µm (Zhao 2022 HD4100) and 15 µm (ABF GY11 per layer). Lamination flow control, re-drill registration and seed adhesion on polymer are the main process limiters.

### Cited Findings
- **Vacuum lamination (GT):** the glass is silane-treated, then vacuum-laminated with polymer on both sides. Electroless and electrolytic Cu go on the polymer before laser ablation. — [GT dissertation](https://repository.gatech.edu/server/api/core/bitstreams/1c691386-1e2b-48b2-bed0-fc610a84f90c/content). A 2015 GT ECTC paper laminated a thin polymer primer over glass with through-vias. "A significant challenge was optimizing vacuum lamination to eliminate voids and improve adhesion of the primer films" (snippet-level). — [academia.edu: Novel Cu metallization schemes on ultra-thin bare glass](https://www.academia.edu/88424636/Novel_copper_metallization_schemes_on_ultra_thin_bare_glass_interposers_with_through_vias)
- **Flow control:** in the via-first process, a 5 µm polymer must be partially pre-cured so that lamination produces a tented via rather than polymer flowing into the vias. Vias are then opened by plasma etch, CO2 laser or UV laser, on 100 µm glass with 10–20 µm vias at 40 µm pitch. — [ResearchGate 313466210](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/313466210_Via-First_Process_to_Enable_Copper_Metallization_of_Glass_Interposers_With_High-Aspect-Ratio_Fine-Pitch_Through-Package-Vias)
- **Residue:** with 5 µm ABF GX92P laminated on both sides, vias did not fully open because of photoresist residue after cure. — [GT high-pitch TPV paper](https://smtnet.com/library/files/upload/high-pitch-vias.pdf)
- **Cure shrinkage:** in GT glass-panel embedding with two 15 µm ABF GY11 layers per side, embedded dies drifted as the polymer shrank on cure, which lowered yield. — [IMAPS 2018 GPE](https://meridian.allenpress.com/ism/article/2018/1/000331/9472/Design-and-demonstration-of-Glass-Panel-Embedding)
- **Via-in-via (fill + re-drill):** the GT patent fills vias with polymer, re-opens smaller holes through the polymer, then seeds and plates. The remaining polymer annulus is the stress buffer. — [US9275934](https://patents.google.com/patent/US9275934). Zhao et al. 2022 propose a "controlled method for filling polymers in TGV". One search summary says the paper flags offset (misregistration of the re-drilled hole) and residual polymer in the via as risks (snippet-level). — [Micromachines 13(10):1799](https://www.mdpi.com/2072-666X/13/10/1799)
- **Parylene CVD conformality (TSV analogue):** Cu-filled TSVs used a 1 µm Parylene-HT liner at AR up to 10 in 100 µm wafers. The liner was "pinhole-free, highly uniform, conformal" and deposited at room temperature, with >80% step coverage for the liner and the barrier/seed. — [ResearchGate 304421563 (Tung, Cheng et al.)](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/304421563_Copper_filled_TSV_formation_with_Parylene-HT_insulator_for_low-temperature_compatible_3D_integration); [ResearchGate 282177249](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/282177249_Fabrication_and_electrical_characterization_of_Parylene-HT_liner_bottom-up_copper_filled_through_silicon_via_TSV). An earlier TSV study reported ~80% step coverage at the lower sidewall for a ~1000 nm parylene film, compared favorably with SiO2. The exact paper in this result set is (unverified). — [ResearchGate 224319250 (Parylene N for TSV)](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/224319250_Parylene_N_as_a_dielectric_material_for_through_silicon_vias)
- **Parylene in deep cavities:** a patent reports confirmed parylene coverage in 350–450 µm-deep cavities. The mechanism is that at low pressure the mean free path exceeds the feature size (snippet-level). — [USPTO 7538032](https://image-ppubs.uspto.gov/dirsearch-public/print/downloadPdf/7538032)
- **Vendor claims for AF4 in TGVs:** room-temperature CVD with "no thermal load" on the substrate and "very low intrinsic stress". The vendor argues that ALD oxides coat vias conformally but lack low k, and that sputtered films lack sidewall coverage (vendor claim). — [COAT-X](https://coat-x.com/parylene-af4-the-dielectric-polymer-redefining-through-glass-via-technology/)
- **Spin / vacuum-assisted fill (BCB, TSV analogue):** vacuum-assisted spin coating gave void-free BCB fill of high-AR annular trenches, which is the basis for annular polymer-isolated TSVs. — [Microelectron. Reliab. 2012 (S0026271412001850)](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0026271412001850); [ResearchGate 256815403](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/256815403_Benzocyclobutene_polymer_filling_of_high_aspect-ratio_annular_trenches_for_fabrication_of_Through-Silicon-Vias_TSVs)
- **Polyimide liner, high AR (TSV):** an "innovative polyimide liner deposition method" reached fills at AR 24:1, and an electrografted polymer liner reached AR 18:1 (snippet-level). — [Microelectron. Eng. 2015 (S0167931715300551)](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0167931715300551)
- **Liner-before-Cu with polyimide precursors:** a patent embodiment coats and cures a polymer such as HD-2610/HD-2611/Durimide on the via wall before Cu. — [US 12087623](https://image-ppubs.uspto.gov/dirsearch-public/print/downloadPdf/12087623)
- **Adhesion promoters:** silane coupling agents were used on bare glass before polymer lamination (GT). — [GT dissertation](https://repository.gatech.edu/server/api/core/bitstreams/1c691386-1e2b-48b2-bed0-fc610a84f90c/content). - **Reported buffer thicknesses in TGV studies:** 1 µm organic layer (电子与封装 070103) — [researching.cn](https://m.researching.cn/articles/OJb7b8695062740314); 5 µm polymer primer — [TCPMT 2017](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/313466210_Via-First_Process_to_Enable_Copper_Metallization_of_Glass_Interposers_With_High-Aspect-Ratio_Fine-Pitch_Through-Package-Vias); 10 µm HD4100 — [Chips review citing Zhao 2022](https://www.mdpi.com/2674-0729/4/3/37); 15 µm ABF GY11 per layer — [IMAPS 2018](https://meridian.allenpress.com/ism/article/2018/1/000331/9472/Design-and-demonstration-of-Glass-Panel-Embedding). For TSVs, "2–5 µm polymer buffer layer" is cited as substantially reducing thermal stress (snippet-level). — [ResearchGate 273394670 BCB-liner TSV](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/273394670_Thermal_and_Electrical_Properties_of_BCB-Liner_Through-Silicon_Vias)

### Inferences
- TGVs are through-holes that are often tapered or hourglass-shaped, open at both ends. Vapor-phase parylene should therefore coat from both openings, which would ease the AR limit compared with blind TSVs. This is a physical inference; I found no TGV-specific step-coverage data. TGV AR is commonly 3–12 (snippet-level). — [Sensors 24(1):171 review](https://www.mdpi.com/1424-8220/24/1/171)
- The practical thickness window seems to be about 1–10 µm. Below ~1 µm, the compliance benefit drops off (from the FE trends in Q3). Above ~5–10 µm on vias smaller than 30 µm, Cu cross-section loss becomes large (see Q4).
- Lamination and via-in-via suit panels (dry films, standard PCB/substrate tools). CVD parylene is batch vacuum: conformal, but slow, and it coats both surfaces, so it would need patterning or removal from the field. Throughput figures were not found.

### Gaps
- No measured parylene, BCB or PI thickness-uniformity data inside glass through-vias (top/middle/bottom) was found for any diameter or AR.
- No panel-scale (510 × 515 mm) uniformity data for any polymer liner in TGVs.
- No data on re-drill registration tolerance (offset) for via-in-via in glass.
- No data on plasma-activation recipes or quantified polymer-to-glass adhesion (e.g., peel strength or Gc) for TGV liners.

## Q3. Quantitative stress-reduction and reliability results: what is demonstrated experimentally and what is only simulated

### Takeaway
Simulations consistently report large reductions. Zhao 2022 found −51% stress at the TGV edge and −69% at the RDL edge with a 10 µm HD4100 PI buffer. 电子与封装 070103 found about −40% glass max principal stress with a 1 µm organic layer. A review quotes ">75% ERR reduction vs solid vias" for a compliant polymer liner. The experimental record is thin, mostly GT daisy chains passing 1000 cycles at −55/125 °C. Demir TDMR 2017 showed that the polymer layers eliminated delamination and cracks that appeared in bare-glass via-first controls. No experimental 400 °C-anneal crack-suppression, Cu-protrusion or KOZ data for polymer buffers was found. The Hanyang "Parylene, ~73% fracture-probability reduction" MRS 2026 claim could not be located.

### Cited Findings
**Simulation (SIM)**

### Inferences
- **Evidence ladder:**
  - EXP with a polymer present: GT ZIF/RXP-4/epoxy/ABF laminates and via-first designs, roughly 2013–2017. Demonstrated outcomes are 1000 cycles at −55/125 °C passed, and no delamination or cracks versus a bare-glass control.
  - EXP with a polymer as an *isolated* variable in a Cu-filled TGV: only Demir TDMR 2017 (polymer vs bare glass) and the 电子与封装 070103 experiment (details unknown).
  - SIM: all of the % reductions quoted above.
- **No experimental data found on** polymer buffers and 400 °C anneal crack suppression, thermal shock, Cu-protrusion reduction or keep-out-zone (KOZ) reduction for TGVs. These claims remain simulation-only or absent.
- The %-reduction figures are not comparable across studies. They use different metrics (edge von Mises or shear vs. max principal stress vs. ERR), locations (TGV edge vs. RDL edge vs. glass bulk) and loads (steady-state cooling vs. heating). Zhao's own data suggest the buffer can *move* the hotspot: lower stress at the RDL edge, possibly higher at the TGV edge in some configurations.
- The FE trend agrees with fracture mechanics. The ERR for radial cracks scales with via diameter × mismatch strain² (Ahmed; Sharma), so a compliant annulus that lowers the Cu-to-glass radial traction lowers ERR most for large vias. Buffers therefore matter most for vias of about 30 µm and larger, the size range that 与非网 reports as having markedly higher risk. — [eefocus](https://www.eefocus.com/article/2025292.html)

## Q4. Problems and limitations: thermal budget, CTE, moisture, adhesion and seed, Cu cross-section, RF loss, cost and reliability-data scarcity

### Takeaway
Polymer buffers work through low modulus (about 2.5–3.5 GPa against roughly 70 GPa for glass and roughly 120 GPa for Cu), not through CTE matching. Their CTE (BCB 42 ppm/K; parylene and epoxy similar or higher) is larger than Cu's (about 17) and glass's (about 3–9), which creates new mismatch and outgassing and moisture paths. Most polymers cannot survive the 400–420 °C Cu anneals used for crack-free TGVs. The exceptions are Parylene HT/AF4 (350 °C continuous, 450 °C peak) and possibly BCB (Tg >350 °C) and PI (HD4100 Tg 320 °C). Liners also take up via cross-section, add dielectric loss (low for BCB/AF4, high for ABF or solder-resist-class materials) and add process steps. The bigger problem is that published reliability data are sparse and in places self-contradictory.

### Inferences
- **Cu cross-section and resistance penalty** (my arithmetic, assuming a uniform annular liner of thickness t in a cylindrical via of diameter D, with R ∝ 1/(D−2t)²):

  | Via D (µm) | Liner t (µm) | Cu area left | DC resistance |
  |---|---|---|---|
  | 50 | 1 | 92% | +8.5% |
  | 50 | 5 | 64% | +56% |
  | 20 | 1 | 81% | +23% |
  | 20 | 2 | 64% | +56% |
  | 10 | 2 | 36% | +178% |

  This is why buffers on sub-20 µm vias are impractical unless they are under 1 µm, where compliance gains shrink. A polymer annulus also adds thermal resistance, since polymer thermal conductivity is typically 0.1–0.3 W/m·K versus about 1 W/m·K for glass (general knowledge; not sourced here, so treat as (unverified)). This erodes the thermal-via function of Cu TGVs.
- **Thermal-budget conflict:** Corning's crack-free anneal route uses about 420 °C. Only Parylene HT/AF4 (450 °C short-term) nominally tolerates that. BCB (Tg >350 °C) and HD4100 (Tg 320 °C) would be above Tg, and ABF/epoxy laminates (Tg typically below about 200 °C, not sourced here) would degrade. The practical consequence is that polymer-buffered TGVs push the Cu anneal down to ≤250–300 °C, or require the Cu to be annealed before the polymer is applied (not possible for a liner). That trade-off may leave higher residual Cu stress and protrusion later.
- **Hermeticity:** polymer liners and fills are permeable. For MEMS or RF hermetic lids, which is Corning's target market, polymer buffers conflict with hermetic fully-filled designs. This is an inference from patent titles and polymer permeability.
- **Mismatch direction:** on heating, a high-CTE polymer annulus (40–60 ppm/K) between Cu (17) and glass (3–9) expands more than either. It can push radially on the glass, tensile-hoop loading it much like Cu does. It escapes trouble only because its modulus is about 1/40 of Cu's. Thick, high-CTE, stiff polymers (e.g., filled epoxies) could lose the benefit, consistent with Zhao's configuration-dependent TGV-edge increase. Le & Choa's preference for low-CTE, moderate-stiffness GZ-41 points the same way.
- **Data conflicts:**
  - (i) Zhao 2022 reports a 51% TGV-edge reduction for HD4100 in one reading and higher TGV-edge stress in another configuration.
  - (ii) Inorganic TiO2 buffers (IME-CAS) report larger reductions (−66.6%) than the 1 µm organic layer (−40%, GDUT), against the intuition that soft polymers should be better.
  - (iii) Le & Choa find that Cu content barely changes ERR, while Zhao finds that stress rises with Cu fill fraction. The metrics differ (edge-crack ERR vs. in-via stress).

### Gaps
- No measured Df/Dk at 28–110 GHz for parylene-, BCB- or PI-lined TGVs, and no insertion-loss comparison of lined against unlined TGVs.
- No published cost or throughput numbers for parylene CVD, lamination plus re-drill, or BCB fill at panel scale.
- No outgassing or blistering data for polymer-lined TGVs during 260 °C reflow, and no moisture-sensitivity-level (MSL) or HAST data beyond the GT 96 h HAST.
- ABF GL102/GZ-41 datasheet CTE, Tg, modulus and Df values could not be retrieved.
- No independent measurement of the CTE of Parylene HT/AF4 (often quoted at about 35–36 ppm/K) was found. Treat it as (unverified).
